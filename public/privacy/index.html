This notice is written to be read on its own, in plain language, and to be understandable without reference to any other document we publish. It describes what personal data we handle, for which specific purpose, who else receives it, how long we keep it, and how you tell us to stop. It is incorporated into and forms part of the Terms of Service.

We prepared this notice by reference to the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, which are being commenced in phases through 2027. The commitments below are the ones we apply now, in every deployment, and not only once those provisions are in force.

1. Who we are, and your two possible relationships with us

Archagentix Private Limited is a company incorporated in Pune, Maharashtra, India. We host and operate Software Factory for customers who subscribe at https://platform.archagentix.com/, and we also distribute it so that an organization can run it inside its own cloud project or on its own machine.

The two cases differ in who decides why data is processed:

  • The hosted service. We are the Data Fiduciary for your account, billing, security and service-usage data, because we decide why that data is processed. For the workspace content you put into the service — prompts, plans, tickets, source code and generated output — we process it on your instructions as your Data Processor, and only to deliver the features you have configured.
  • Your own deployment. The organization or individual running it decides why and how information is processed, which optional services are configured, where data is hosted, and which retention and access policies apply. That operator is the Data Fiduciary, and this notice describes the software's design rather than replacing that operator's own disclosures to its users.

Contact details for both cases are in section 16.

3. Consent, and how you take it back

Where we rely on your consent, we ask for it before the processing starts, with the purposes in section 2 presented itemised rather than as one bundled acceptance. You give consent when you create an account and when you connect each third-party service, and each of those is a separate choice.

Withdrawal is as easy as giving it. Analytics is one click in Cookie Preferences. Disconnecting a tracker, source-code host or model provider is one click in Settings next to that integration. Closing the whole account is in Settings → Billing and Settings → Organization. Withdrawing consent stops the processing that rested on it from that moment; it does not undo processing that already happened, and it does not affect processing we carry out as your Data Processor on your instructions.

Some processing we treat as a legitimate use rather than a consent matter, because it protects you, us, or the contract we have with you: authentication, access control, fraud and abuse prevention, security logging, billing, tax record-keeping, and responding to your requests. Where we rely on this, we limit it to those activities.

4. Identity and authentication data

In a deployment that requires sign-in, Software Factory uses Firebase Authentication with enabled identity providers such as Google or Microsoft. The software may receive and use an account subject identifier, display name, email address, email-verification status, profile image, authentication provider, and organization role or permissions. Authentication tokens are used to establish and verify a session and authorize workspace requests.

We store an opaque account identifier, not your password. The configured identity provider and Firebase process authentication data under their own terms and privacy notices, and the choice of provider is theirs, not ours.

5. Workspace and operational data

Software Factory processes data that you, your organization, or connected services make available to it. Depending on configuration and use, this can include:

  • organization, member, role, project, ticket, milestone, and workflow data;
  • prompts, instructions, plans, call transcripts or notes, conversation history, agent messages, approvals, and AI-generated output;
  • repository metadata and the source code or other files needed for an agent task;
  • model, runtime, connector, deployment, and workspace settings; and
  • job status, timestamps, errors, audit information, traces, token usage, latency, cost, and other operational logs.

We use this information to provide the requested planning, coding, testing, deployment, collaboration, troubleshooting, and reporting features. Autonomous agents may create or update records in connected project trackers and source-code hosts when instructed and authorized to do so.

Model providers and your content. When you route a task to a hosted model provider, the prompt and the repository or ticket content for that task go to that provider so it can generate the answer. That transfer is the service you asked for. Providers process under their own terms and generally retain little or nothing for API traffic, but they are outside our control; you choose the provider per role, and a local model keeps inference on your own machine.

6. Credentials and security safeguards

Users and operators configure API keys, OAuth tokens, repository and tracker credentials, and model-provider credentials. The design keeps those secrets server-side, encrypts them at rest in an organization-scoped vault, and returns only masked presence information to the browser. An egress proxy injects a credential into an authorized outbound request outside the agent's own sandbox, so an agent container never holds a raw provider key.

The safeguards we maintain over personal data are:

  • Encryption of personal data in transit (TLS) and of stored credentials and organization secrets at rest, with masking of card numbers and secret values wherever they are displayed;
  • Access control to the computer resources that hold personal data, limited to the identities a role needs, with the shared-secret and workload-identity boundaries described in the Terms;
  • Logging, monitoring and periodic review sufficient to detect, investigate and remediate a breach;
  • Continuity measures, including scheduled backups of the operational data store, so that a lost or corrupted record can be restored;
  • Retention of security logs and of the data needed to investigate an incident for one year, after which they are erased unless a specific legal obligation requires longer; and
  • Written security obligations on every Data Processor we engage, listed in section 9.

No system can guarantee absolute security. Promptly revoke or rotate a credential if you believe it has been exposed, and tell us at the address in section 16 so we can act on it.

7. Browser storage, cookies and essential functionality

The browser uses local storage and authentication storage for essential and preference features. These records can include theme and language choices, sidebar state, the selected organization or project, the last workspace route, organization-scoped workflow drafts, and authentication state maintained by the configured sign-in provider. Essential functionality cannot be turned off through Cookie Preferences because the application may not work correctly without it.

The analytics choice is stored separately in local storage as ai-fleet.analytics-consent, with a value of enabled or disabled. That key keeps the product's earlier name deliberately: your recorded choice must survive a rebrand, so we do not rename the place where it is stored. Clearing browser data removes these preferences and causes default behavior to apply again.

8. Optional Google Analytics 4 telemetry

An operator may configure an optional Google Analytics 4 (GA4) web data stream. If no valid GA4 measurement ID is configured, the Google tag is not downloaded and no GA4 page view is sent. When GA4 is configured, page-view measurement is on by default and you switch it off in Cookie Preferences; the moment you do, the tag stops loading and a consent-revocation update is sent to GA4 if the tag was already loaded.

The integration sends a normalized top-level route, a generic page title, and an anonymous or authenticated status for page-view measurement. For a signed-in Firebase user, it can configure GA4 with the stable, opaque account subject as Google's user_id. It does not send email addresses, display names, organization or project identifiers, invitation tokens, browser query strings, or dynamic UI titles. Google may set _ga and _ga_* cookies and process the resulting telemetry under the Google Privacy Policy.

Disabling Analytics persists the opt-out, stops future page views, expires GA cookies accessible to the application, and reloads the same application route. On later startup, the application does not create Google analytics globals or download the Google tag while the stored choice remains disabled. An opt-out does not remove data that Google or the deployment operator received before the choice changed.

Data goes to a third party only as needed for features selected and configured by a user or deployment operator. Each of the following is a Data Processor or, where you connect it yourself, a service you choose directly:

  • Firebase, Google, or Microsoft for identity and authentication;
  • Google Cloud services for hosting, databases, messaging, secrets, and jobs;
  • hosted LLM providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google, or a local model provider selected for a task;
  • project trackers such as Linear, Jira, or Asana;
  • source-code hosts such as GitHub or GitLab;
  • Stripe and Flexprice for card payment, subscription and invoice management;
  • Langfuse for optional tracing and operational analytics;
  • Google Analytics for optional website telemetry; and
  • search or other tools explicitly enabled for an agent workflow.

We hold a written agreement with each processor we engage for our own service that obliges it to secure the personal data it handles, to process it only on our instructions, and to assist with breach notification and data-principal requests. Third-party services you connect directly are governed by their own terms, locations, retention and security practices.

10. Transfers of personal data outside India

The hosted service runs on Google Cloud, and the platform's compute and storage are currently provisioned in Google Cloud regions outside India (including us-central1), with model providers and other processors typically located outside India as well. Personal data therefore crosses borders as an ordinary part of the service.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 permits transfer of personal data for processing to any country unless the Central Government restricts that country by notification, and the Rules, 2025 confirm transfer outside India is permitted subject to any requirement the Central Government specifies. If a restriction is notified that affects our arrangements, we will tell affected customers and adjust the deployment rather than continue the transfer. If your own obligations require data to stay in India, an Enterprise deployment can be provisioned inside your own Indian cloud project, where you choose the regions.

11. How long we keep it, and deletion

We keep personal data only while the purpose in section 2 that justifies it is still being served, and we erase it when that purpose ends or when you withdraw consent and no other ground applies. Specifically:

  • During your subscription: account, workspace, billing and audit records are kept so the service can do its job.
  • After cancellation: you can export your workspace for 30 days, as the Refund and Cancellation Policy states. We then delete account, workspace and operational records within 30 days of that window closing.
  • Invoices and payment records: kept for the period Indian tax and company law requires (eight years for books of account), and not for any other purpose.
  • Security logs and incident records: one year, as section 6 states.
  • Browser preferences: until you replace them or clear browser storage.
  • Content you delete: deleted from the active workspace promptly. Backups are overwritten as they roll off on their normal schedule, so a small number of records may persist briefly in backup and are not restored except to recover from a failure.

Deletion of what we hold does not delete a copy already delivered to a connected tracker, source-code host or model provider under your own configuration; those are governed by that service.

12. Your rights, and how to exercise each

Under the Act you may ask us to:

  • access information about the personal data we process about you, and the identities of the processors it went to (section 11 of the Act);
  • correct or complete inaccurate or misleading personal data, and erase data that is no longer needed for the purpose in section 2 (section 12 of the Act);
  • withdraw consent at any time (section 6(4) of the Act), as section 3 above describes;
  • nominate another person to exercise your rights in the event of death or incapacity (section 14 of the Act); and
  • grieve to us first, and then to the Data Protection Board of India if we do not resolve it (section 13 of the Act).

How to make a request. Email support@archagentix.com with the subject line "Data request", or write to the postal address in section 16. For an in-workspace request you do not need to contact anyone at all: export, correction and deletion are available in Settings under your own account, and analytics and connector choices in Cookie Preferences and Settings.

What we need from you. We must verify you are the account holder before disclosing or changing anything, so include the email address on the account, or your account's subject identifier or username as shown in Settings, and the organization name if the request concerns workspace data. We will tell you if we need more to identify you. A request can be made to us only in respect of data you consented to with us; for your own deployment, address it to that operator.

Our timeline. We acknowledge a request within 3 business days and substantively respond within 30 days. The Rules, 2025 permit a grievance response window of up to ninety days; we hold ourselves to thirty. If a request is complex we will tell you the reason for delay and the expected date within that 30-day period. Requests to our service are free of charge.

Complaining to the Board. If you are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India, established under the Act, through its published portal at the address the Board specifies. We will include that route in every response we send you about a rights request.

13. Children

Software Factory is a business tool for registered organizations and adult users. "Child" means an individual who has not completed eighteen years of age, and we do not intend to process the personal data of children. Our sign-in requires a business or work account and the Terms may only be accepted by an adult, which is the due diligence we perform to confirm a Data Principal is not a child.

We do not track or behaviourally monitor children, and we do not direct targeted advertising at them. If we learn that a child's personal data has been processed, we erase it, except where a limited purpose or exemption permits us to keep it. If you believe a child has given us personal data, tell us at section 16 and we will act on it.

14. If our security fails: breach notification

Becoming aware of a personal data breach triggers two obligations, and we commit to both:

  1. To you, without delay, through the email or in-app channel you registered with us, in concise plain language: what happened, including its nature, extent and timing; the consequences likely for you; what we have done and are doing to contain and mitigate it; what you can do to protect yourself; and the business contact details of the person who can answer your questions about it.
  2. To the Data Protection Board of India: an initial intimation without delay, and a detailed report — including the facts and causes, mitigation, findings about who caused the breach, remedial measures to prevent recurrence, and a record of the intimations sent to affected individuals — within seventy-two hours of becoming aware, or any longer period the Board allows on our written request.

Where a deployment is run by an operator rather than by us, the operator is the Data Fiduciary and owns these notifications; we tell the operator without delay and help with the information the notification needs.

Continued use after the change date means the new notice applies to processing from then on.
